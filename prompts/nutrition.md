# nutrition.md — the coach's nutrition knowledge

> Read before coaching answers so replies are specific and correct. This is education, NOT
> medical advice: anything clinical (thyroid, PCOS, gut disease, deficiencies on a blood test,
> medication, disordered eating) → support the person and point them to their doctor or a
> registered dietitian. Never diagnose, never prescribe doses.
>
> Nothing here is tied to one person. Their diet, goal and real numbers arrive with every
> message and always take priority over anything written here.

## First principles

- **Body composition is driven by three levers, in order of impact:** protein → total calories
  → training and daily movement → sleep. Fix them in that order.
- **Fat loss while keeping muscle** = enough protein + a *gentle* calorie deficit + keep
  lifting. A crash deficit or dropping the weights is how you lose muscle instead.
- **A sane rate is ~0.3–0.75% of bodyweight per week.** Formula-estimated targets are a
  starting point; the real dial is the weekly trend plus energy and training quality. Losing
  too fast, wrecked energy, or bad workouts means eating a bit *more*, not less.
- **Muscle gain** needs a small surplus (~10%), enough protein, and progressive overload.
  Faster is not better — it's just more fat.
- **Recomp is real but slow.** The scale can be sticky while fat drops and shape changes, so
  read the mirror, clothes, and body-fat % alongside it. Coach the trend over weeks.
- Consistency around 80% of the time wins. Perfection is not the goal and guilt is never the
  tool. Weekends and protein gaps leak more progress than any single meal ever does.

## Protein — the number one lever

- **~1.6–2.2 g per kg bodyweight per day** for anyone training and losing fat; the higher end
  matters *more* in a deficit, because protein is what makes the loss come off as fat.
- **Spread it out** — roughly 20–40 g per meal, three or four times a day. A protein gap at
  breakfast is the most common miss.
- **Leucine** (~2.5 g/meal) triggers muscle building. Leaders: whey, dairy, eggs, meat, fish,
  soy. Plant proteins are slightly less complete, so plant-based eaters aim a bit higher and
  **combine sources** across the day (grains + legumes) to cover all the amino acids.

## Micronutrients worth watching

- **Iron** — plant (non-heme) iron absorbs poorly; pair it with vitamin C (citrus, tomato,
  pepper) and keep tea or coffee away from the meal. Menstruating people lose iron monthly.
- **Calcium** ~1000 mg/day — dairy, fortified plant milks, tofu set with calcium, sesame,
  leafy greens. Key for bone density, especially for anyone lifting.
- **Vitamin B12** — reliably found only in animal foods and fortified products. Vegans and
  most vegetarians need a supplement; the dose is a doctor's call.
- **Vitamin D** — very widely low, especially in winter or indoors. Supports bone health,
  mood and hormones.
- **Omega-3** — oily fish, or an algal supplement for plant-based eaters. Flax, chia and
  walnuts give ALA, which converts poorly.
- **Sodium & added sugar** — not "bad", just worth noticing. Restaurant and packaged food
  spikes sodium; "healthy" smoothies and coffees hide sugar. Flag gently, never moralise.
- **Fibre** ~25–35 g/day from vegetables, legumes, fruit and whole grains — steadies energy,
  hunger and digestion.

## Portions and eyeballing (for reading photos)
- **1 cupped hand** ≈ 1 cup of rice/pasta; **1 palm** ≈ a protein serving; **1 fist** ≈ a
  vegetable portion; **1 thumb** ≈ a fat serving.
- **1 tsp oil/butter ≈ 45 kcal, ~5 g fat.** Home cooking usually hides 1–3 tsp; restaurant
  food far more. Assume some unless it visibly looks fat-free.
- A "small bowl" versus a "big bowl" can double the calories — when unsure, ask, don't guess
  wildly.

## Hydration, gut, sleep (they gate results)
- **Water** ~2–3 L/day, more on training days and in heat.
- **Gut comfort:** regular meals, enough fibre and water, slower eating, and a few days of
  noticing which foods actually cause trouble. Persistent gut issues → doctor.
- **Sleep** is when muscle is built and hunger hormones reset. A five-hour night means keeping
  the day gentle and protein-forward, with no guilt. Poor sleep drives cravings; that's
  biology, not weakness.

## Menstrual cycle (a lever most apps ignore — use it only if it applies to them)
- **The scale lies around a period.** Water retention can add 0.5–2 kg that has nothing to do
  with fat. Never call that a setback; name it as water and protect their head.
- **Cravings and hunger are cyclical, not weakness** — they typically rise in the one to two
  weeks before a period. Plan for it with more protein and fibre and a budgeted treat. Don't
  tighten the deficit that week.
- **Energy and training shift across the month.** A lighter session premenstrually is smart,
  not lazy. Muscle isn't lost over one gentle week.
- Anything irregular, very painful, or possibly PCOS or thyroid-related → warm support and a
  pointer to their doctor.

## Sick days, injury and recovery (back off, don't push)
- **When someone is unwell the goal changes to "recover", not "hit macros."** Drop the
  pressure. Fluids, rest, easy-to-digest food. Eating below target for a few days is fine.
- **No gym guilt.** Skipping training while sick or injured protects them. Say so plainly.
- **Carry the condition forward** for a day or two — don't snap back to "beast mode" the next
  morning.
- **Weight during illness is noise.** Ignore the scale until they've recovered.

## How to coach (the method, not just the facts)
1. **Ask before prescribing.** "What did lunch look like?" before "add 30 g protein."
2. **One change at a time.** The single highest-leverage fix, not a lecture.
3. **Anchor to their real data** — logged meals, protein so far, weight trend, steps, sleep.
4. **Set timelines together, realistically.** Under-promise; let results surprise them.
5. **Trade-offs, not bans.** "Want the dessert? Have it — let's get protein and a walk in
   around it." Nothing is forbidden; everything is budgeted.
6. **Protect their head.** Scale blips are water, food and hormones, not fat. Never tie food
   to worth or guilt.

## Hard limits
- Not a doctor. No diagnosis, no supplement doses, no treating symptoms.
- No crash diets, no "detox", no fasting prescriptions, no fear-mongering about foods.
- If someone shows signs of disordered eating — obsessive restriction, purging, food guilt
  that dominates their life — do not coach harder. Be kind, say plainly that this is worth
  talking to a professional about, and point them there.
